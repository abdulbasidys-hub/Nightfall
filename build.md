# NIGHTFALL WEBSITE --- BUILD SPECIFICATION

**Project:** Nightfall\
**Document:** Build specification for the implementation agent\
**Version:** 1.0\
**Status:** Approved direction; build-ready\
**Primary market:** Web3 and crypto, with a broader multidisciplinary
digital-execution offering

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Project objective

Build a distinctive, premium, multi-page website for **Nightfall**, a
Multidisciplinary Digital Execution Studio.

The website must communicate that Nightfall can take ideas from concept
to execution across creative, technical, digital-product, automation,
and growth work. Web3 and crypto are the initial market and strongest
portfolio focus, but the brand should not look limited to crypto.

The site must feel deliberately art-directed, confident, experimental,
and technically capable. It must not resemble: - A generic
digital-agency template. - A token or memecoin website. - A gaming clan
or esports brand. - A generic AI-generated landing page. - A one-page
brochure with every section crammed together.

The result should feel like a carefully designed digital studio with its
own visual language.

## 2. Approved creative direction

### Brand personality

-   Bold and aggressive, but controlled.
-   Confident, inventive, technically capable.
-   Creative without looking chaotic.
-   Premium without becoming sterile.

### Colour system

Use a black, green, silver, and white palette. The site must **not** be
dark from top to bottom. Alternate dark and light sections to create
rhythm and contrast.

Suggested tokens: - Obsidian: `#080A0A` - Electric green: `#00E676` -
Silver: `#C5CBC8` - White: `#F5F7F6` - Graphite: `#202523`

Use green as a deliberate accent, not as a flood fill. Use white and
light-grey surfaces for editorial contrast. Check all text/background
combinations for accessible contrast.

### Typography

Suggested pairing: - Display/headings: Space Grotesk or Sora. - Body/UI:
Inter.

Use large, expressive display typography where it serves the
composition. Avoid making every section a giant text poster. Keep
paragraphs readable and limit unnecessary copy.

### Art direction and composition

Combine cinematic, editorial, and experimental approaches: -
Asymmetrical grids and intentional negative space. - Oversized but
selective typography. - Large project imagery and immersive previews. -
Alternating dark and light surfaces. - Strong section-to-section
transitions. - Distinct layouts for different kinds of work. -
Controlled use of green, silver, and geometric forms.

Avoid gratuitous gradients, glassmorphism, random crypto symbols,
decorative noise, excessive borders, and generic stock imagery.

## 3. Character / mascot usage

Nightfall has an original anime-inspired male character: - Black hair. -
Calm, confident, slightly mysterious expression. - No accessories. -
Clean, recognizable face and silhouette.

The character may appear sparingly in selected moments, such as the
homepage hero, About page, or a carefully designed transition. Do not
place the character everywhere or make the website feel like an anime
brand. Preserve the character's appearance consistently when using
supplied character artwork. Do not invent a new character design.

## 4. Site architecture and navigation

This is a **multi-page website**, not a single-page landing page.

Primary navigation: 1. Home 2. Work 3. Services 4. About 5. Contact

Required routes: - `/` --- Homepage - `/work` --- Portfolio index -
`/work/[slug]` --- Individual project page - `/services` --- Services -
`/about` --- About Nightfall - `/contact` --- Contact

Use a responsive navigation system. Desktop navigation may be expressive
and minimal; mobile navigation must remain fast, obvious, and easy to
use.

## 5. Page requirements

### 5.1 Homepage

The homepage should be an experience and a strong introduction, not a
long company brochure.

Recommended sequence: 1. **Hero:** A distinctive statement of what
Nightfall does, a clear route to selected work, and a strong visual
moment. Use animated typography or a carefully art-directed composition.
Character artwork may be used here sparingly. 2. **Introduction:** A
concise explanation of Nightfall as a multidisciplinary digital
execution studio. 3. **Selected work:** A small, curated selection of
portfolio projects, with varied compositions rather than identical
cards. 4. **Capabilities:** Introduce the major capability groups with
links to Services. 5. **Execution approach:** Show how Nightfall moves
from idea to execution. Keep it concise and visual. 6. **Closing CTA:**
Invite visitors to discuss a project or collaboration.

Do not make up client names, project outcomes, performance figures,
testimonials, or business metrics.

### 5.2 Work / portfolio index

The portfolio should feel like an **interactive digital exhibition**,
not a standard grid of identical cards.

Use two complementary presentation modes:

**Featured Projects** - Large, immersive project compositions. - Vary
the layout and visual treatment by project. - PULSE may receive a
prominent showcase with a product preview and supporting brand
visuals. - Each project should communicate what it is and what Nightfall
contributed.

**Creative Archive** - An asymmetric visual wall for graphics, banners,
memes, short-form creative, website previews, and other smaller
pieces. - Mix image sizes and media formats intentionally. - Category
filters should be useful without flattening the visual experience into a
generic grid.

Each portfolio item must link to an individual project page where
appropriate.

Suggested categories: - Web3 & Crypto - Websites & Digital Experiences -
Creative & Design - Automation & Systems - Product & Development -
Growth & Campaign Execution

Only show categories that contain published work. Do not create empty
category pages or imply capability through fabricated projects.

### 5.3 Individual project pages

Do not force every project into one identical case-study template. Each
project should support a presentation type that determines its layout.

Supported presentation types: - `product_showcase` -
`editorial_gallery` - `website_preview` - `video_showcase` -
`case_study` - `interactive_demo`

Examples: - PULSE: product showcase. - A graphic or banner project:
editorial gallery. - A website: website preview with screenshots or a
live preview link. - An automation project: case study or interactive
workflow demonstration. - A video: video showcase.

Each project page should support: - Project title and short summary. -
Project category and tags. - Project classification:
concept/self-initiated or commissioned/client work. - Nightfall's role
and contribution. - Project context or challenge. - Execution/process
details where relevant. - Image and video galleries. - Optional external
project link. - Related projects. - A route back to Work and a contact
CTA.

Only display fields that have been supplied. Never fabricate client
approval, results, metrics, testimonials, or live functionality.

### 5.4 Services page

Present Nightfall's capabilities clearly without making the page look
like a generic list of agency packages.

Capability groups:

**Creative & Design** - Web3 and crypto graphics. - Banners and
promotional assets. - Memes and visual content. - Brand identity and
visual systems. - Content creative for campaigns.

**Websites & Digital Experiences** - Landing pages. - Product and
business websites. - Web3 websites. - Interactive digital experiences.

**Automation & Systems** - Workflow automation. - AI-powered
workflows. - Custom management systems. - Business process optimization.

**Technical Development** - Web applications. - Product development. -
Frontend and backend implementation. - Technical collaboration with
development teams.

**Growth & Execution** - Content creation and posting support. -
Campaign and launch support. - Token/community promotional support. -
Ongoing creative and execution support.

Describe capabilities honestly. Do not promise financial returns, token
price performance, guaranteed growth, or outcomes outside Nightfall's
control.

### 5.5 About page

Explain Nightfall's philosophy and multidisciplinary approach. Focus on
how creative thinking and technical execution work together.

Use the character artwork only if it improves the composition. Avoid
invented history, team size, client roster, awards, or credentials. Do
not imply Nightfall is a large agency or team if that is not accurate.

### 5.6 Contact page

Use **both**: - A proper contact form. - Direct contact options such as
email and WhatsApp.

The form should collect only useful information: - Name. - Email or
preferred contact. - Project/service area. - Short project
description. - Optional budget or timeline, if desired.

Include clear success, validation, and error states. Protect the form
from spam. Contact details must be supplied by the owner before launch;
do not invent email addresses or phone numbers.

## 6. Portfolio CMS and publishing workflow

Use **Sanity CMS** as the content management system so Nightfall can add
and update portfolio work without editing website source code.

Provide a private studio/content-management entry point, for example
`/studio`, protected by authentication. This is the website's chosen
route for the Sanity Studio integration, not a public-facing page.

The CMS must support: - Create, edit, draft, publish, and unpublish
projects. - Upload and manage images and videos. - Set project title,
slug, summary, category, tags, and date. - Set project classification:
`concept` or `commissioned`. - Set Nightfall's role/contribution. -
Select presentation type. - Mark a project as featured. - Set display
order. - Add galleries and optional external links. - Add structured
content sections or blocks for project-specific storytelling. - Preview
a project before publishing.

Suggested project schema:

``` ts
type Project = {
  title: string;
  slug: string;
  summary: string;
  category: string;
  tags?: string[];
  classification: "concept" | "commissioned";
  presentationType:
    | "product_showcase"
    | "editorial_gallery"
    | "website_preview"
    | "video_showcase"
    | "case_study"
    | "interactive_demo";
  featured: boolean;
  displayOrder?: number;
  coverMedia: SanityImage;
  gallery?: MediaItem[];
  role?: string;
  context?: string;
  process?: ContentBlock[];
  outcome?: ContentBlock[];
  externalUrl?: string;
  publishedAt?: string;
};
```

The public site should fetch published content from Sanity. Draft
content must not appear publicly. Configure image delivery and
responsive image sizes appropriately.

## 7. Technical stack

Recommended stack: - **Next.js** with React. - **TypeScript** for
maintainability and safer component/data contracts. - Next.js App
Router. - **Sanity** for portfolio content and media management. -
**GSAP + ScrollTrigger** for complex scroll-driven and timeline
animations. - **Framer Motion** for component-level transitions and
interaction. - **Lenis** only if smooth scrolling materially improves
the experience and does not interfere with accessibility or native
browser behavior. - Use CSS for simpler transitions and effects. - Use
Three.js / React Three Fiber only where a specific visual concept
genuinely benefits from 3D.

Next.js is still React. TypeScript is recommended, not a measure of
professionalism by itself. If the implementation environment or owner
requires JavaScript, the same architecture can be implemented in
JavaScript without changing the design.

Do not add libraries without a clear need. Avoid overlapping animation
systems that compete for control of the same elements.

## 8. Motion and interaction direction

Advanced animation is approved, provided it is purposeful and
performant.

Potential techniques: - Animated hero typography. - Scroll-triggered
section reveals. - Carefully controlled parallax. - Project transitions
and interactive previews. - Horizontal project galleries where
appropriate. - Responsive hover and pointer interactions. - Page
transitions. - Animated graphic elements and background details. -
Character animation in selected moments.

Rules: - Every animation must support hierarchy, storytelling, feedback,
or navigation. - Avoid animating every element. - Avoid long delays
before visitors can use the site. - Keep mobile performance in mind. -
Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. - Provide usable keyboard and touch
interactions. - Do not hijack scrolling or prevent normal browser
navigation. - Ensure content remains readable if animations fail or are
disabled. - Lazy-load heavy media and avoid loading 3D assets unless
needed.

## 9. Responsive design and accessibility

Design and test for: - Large desktop. - Standard laptop. - Tablet. -
Mobile.

Requirements: - No horizontal overflow. - Readable type at all viewport
sizes. - Clear focus states. - Semantic HTML and logical heading
hierarchy. - Keyboard-operable navigation and controls. - Useful alt
text for meaningful images. - Accessible form labels and error
messages. - Sufficient colour contrast. - Reduced-motion support.

Do not simply shrink the desktop layout for mobile. Re-compose complex
sections for narrow screens.

## 10. Performance, SEO, and quality

-   Use Next.js metadata for page titles, descriptions, canonical URLs,
    and social sharing.
-   Create unique metadata for project pages.
-   Add sitemap and robots configuration.
-   Use optimized responsive images.
-   Lazy-load below-the-fold media.
-   Avoid unnecessary client-side JavaScript.
-   Keep the initial hero fast and stable.
-   Prevent layout shifts by reserving media dimensions.
-   Add meaningful 404 and error states.
-   Test all routes and links.
-   Ensure the website works when animations are disabled.

Do not invent SEO claims or keyword-stuff page copy.

## 11. Content and brand guardrails

-   Nightfall is a multidisciplinary digital execution studio.
-   Web3 and crypto are the initial market focus, not the only possible
    market.
-   Do not confuse Nightfall with Nobody Brothers.
-   Do not describe Nightfall as a token, exchange, investment platform,
    or financial service.
-   Do not imply that concept projects are commissioned client work.
-   Label fictional/self-initiated projects clearly.
-   Do not invent clients, testimonials, awards, performance metrics, or
    results.
-   Avoid generic agency copy such as empty claims about being
    "passionate", "innovative", or "the best".
-   Keep copy concise, confident, and specific.

## 12. Implementation approach

Build in stages. Do not attempt to create every page and animation in
one unreviewable pass.

**Stage 1: Foundation** - Set up the Next.js project and TypeScript. -
Establish design tokens, typography, layout primitives, and responsive
rules. - Set up navigation and all required routes. - Establish reusable
components and animation conventions.

**Stage 2: Core pages** - Build homepage. - Build Work index and project
detail route. - Build Services, About, and Contact pages. - Use
realistic placeholder content only where owner-supplied content is not
yet available. Clearly mark placeholders for replacement.

**Stage 3: CMS** - Set up Sanity project and schemas. - Integrate
`/studio` with authentication. - Connect published project data to Work
and project detail pages. - Test draft, publish, unpublish, media
upload, and preview workflows.

**Stage 4: Motion and polish** - Add approved animations after layouts
and responsive behavior are stable. - Check mobile performance and
reduced-motion behavior. - Refine transitions, hover states, and project
presentation layouts.

**Stage 5: Quality assurance and launch readiness** - Test every route
and interaction. - Test forms and validation. - Check accessibility and
responsive layouts. - Check SEO metadata and social previews. - Check
image optimization and performance. - Remove placeholder content that
has not been approved. - Provide a short handover explaining how to run
the project, configure environment variables, manage Sanity content, and
deploy.

## 13. Definition of done

The website is ready for owner review when: - All six page types/routes
work. - Navigation works on desktop and mobile. - Work index loads
published projects from Sanity. - Individual project pages use the
selected presentation type. - The owner can create and publish a project
through the CMS without editing code. - Concept and commissioned work
are clearly distinguished. - Contact form has working validation and
clear submission feedback. - Direct contact options work once owner
details are supplied. - Animations are purposeful, responsive, and
reduced-motion aware. - The website has no fabricated claims or
unapproved placeholder content. - The site is responsive, accessible,
and reasonably performant. - Setup, CMS, and deployment instructions are
included in the handover.

## 14. Owner inputs required before public launch

The implementation can begin without these details, using clearly marked
placeholders. They must be supplied before launch: - Approved Nightfall
logo/wordmark assets, if any. The identity should not depend on a
logo. - Final character artwork files and usage variants. - Final email
address and WhatsApp number. - Final domain and deployment account. -
Approved project images, videos, descriptions, and external links. - Any
final copy or legal/privacy details required for the contact form.

**Important:** Build the visual system and site architecture first. Do
not let missing portfolio assets become an excuse to produce a generic
template. The design quality and interaction system are central
requirements of this project.
